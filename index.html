Multilingual and Explainable Text Detoxification with Parallel Corpora

Extending parallel detoxification datasets to 9 languages, introducing LLM-based explainability analysis, and a novel Chain-of-Thoughts prompting method for text style transfer.

📄 Abstract

Even with various regulations in place across countries and social media platforms, digital abusive speech remains a significant issue. One potential approach is automatic text detoxification—a text style transfer (TST) approach that transforms toxic language into a more neutral form.

We extend the parallel text detoxification corpus to five new languages—German, Chinese, Arabic, Hindi, and Amharic—and test comprehensive multilingual TST baselines. Next, we conduct the first automated, explainable analysis of descriptive features of both toxic and non-toxic sentences, diving deeply into the nuances, similarities, and differences of toxicity and detoxification across 9 languages.

Finally, based on the obtained insights, we experiment with a novel text detoxification method inspired by Chain-of-Thoughts (CoT) reasoning, enhancing the prompting process through clustering on relevant descriptive attributes.

What We Introduce

🌍

Extended Multilingual Corpus

Manual annotation of parallel detoxification datasets for 5 new languages: German, Chinese, Arabic, Hindi, and Amharic — with native speakers and detailed quality control.

🔎

First Explainability Study

First-of-its-kind automated explainability analysis of parallel detoxification data across 9 languages — examining toxicity levels, tone, language type, and implied sentiment.

🧠

Chain-of-Thoughts Detoxification

A novel CoT prompting method using K-means clustering on descriptive attributes to guide LLMs. Outperforms all baselines including standard few-shot GPT-4 prompting.

📊

Comprehensive Benchmarking

Thorough evaluation across unsupervised, supervised (mBART), and LLM-based approaches using STA, SIM (LaBSE), and ChrF1 metrics.

🧠 Method

Chain-of-Thoughts Detoxification

We propose a novel CoT prompting strategy that leverages explainability insights and K-means clustering to guide LLM detoxification with higher precision.

CoT Detoxification Pipeline

1

Feature Extraction

GPT-4 extracts descriptive features (toxicity level, tone, language type, sentiment) for each sentence using the CO-STAR framework.

↓
2

K-means Clustering (k=3)

Sentences one-hot encoded from descriptive features and clustered into 3 groups per language. Cluster 0: Offensive/Hostile/Vulgar · Cluster 1: Condescending/Derogatory · Cluster 2: Informal/Casual/Dismissive

↓
3

Cluster-Guided Prompting

At inference, the LLM identifies the input's cluster, receives a cluster description + representative example, then detoxifies with cluster-specific guidance.

↓
4

Targeted Detoxification

Reduced hallucinations · improved precision · incorporation of cultural specifics · best average Joint score across all methods.

CLUSTER 0
Offensive · Hostile · Vulgar
Strategy: Remove profanities
CLUSTER 1
Condescending · Derogatory
Strategy: Significant rephrasing
CLUSTER 2
Informal · Casual · Dismissive
Strategy: Neutral substitution
